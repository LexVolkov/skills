# Voice card

Five lines written before the draft. It forces attention on the book's actual
cadence instead of the model's default. Keep it in the reply or in private
notes — never as a lore card, because it is a tool, not canon.

## Template

```text
VOICE CARD — <book>, turn <n>
1. POV / tense:
2. Sentence shape:
3. Dialogue:
4. Register / humor:
5. Images / motifs:
Do not copy:
```

## How to fill it

Read the last two or three turns, then:

1. **POV / tense** — who perceives, and whether the book switches by scene.
   Note the separator if it does.
2. **Sentence shape** — roughly count words per sentence. Note comma chains,
   fragments, dashes, and whether paragraphs are long or clipped.
3. **Dialogue** — quote style (`"…"` or `—…`), tag habits, action beats,
   whether characters speak in full sentences.
4. **Register / humor** — grim, light, comic, dry. Note whose interior voice
   carries the humor.
5. **Images / motifs** — repeated objects and senses. Reuse one at most; do
   not turn them into a checklist.

## Filled example

```text
VOICE CARD — Shu and Mercy, turn 5 (after #4)
1. POV / tense: 3rd limited, past; scene switches between Mercy and Shu,
   separated by ---.
2. Sentence shape: Mercy — long comma-chained sentences, domestic detail,
   free indirect thought; Shu — short declaratives, one sensory fact per
   line, occasional fragment.
3. Dialogue: double quotes, sparse tags, action beats; silence used as
   tension.
4. Register: fantasy thriller; dry self-aware humor only in Shu's interior
   voice; Mercy grounded and everyday.
5. Images: fog, forest, mirrors, coffee, copper and blood taste.
Do not copy: typos, comma splices, accidental pronoun slips, the previous
author's pet phrases beyond one echo.
```

## Rules

- Match cadence, not mistakes. Typos and slips are not voice.
- If the prose is rough, stay rough. One polished turn in a rough book reads
  as a foreign body.
- Reuse a motif once per turn, not in every paragraph.
- Rebuild the card when the POV changes, a new chapter starts, a new co-author
  joins, or the Charter tone changes.
- First turn of a book: build the card from the Charter tone and genre. Keep
  the voice plain; a mannered debut is harder to continue.
